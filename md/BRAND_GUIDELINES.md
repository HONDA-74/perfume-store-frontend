## 1. Brand Name & Meaning

### 1.1 The Name

**KENZ** — from the Arabic **كنز** (*kanz*), meaning **"Treasure."**

KENZ is not a fragrance house and does not manufacture perfume. It is a **premium, multi-brand luxury fragrance marketplace** — a curated destination where the world's most respected designer and niche perfume houses (Dior, Chanel, Tom Ford, Giorgio Armani, and niche/artisanal houses such as Creed, Xerjoff, Maison Francis Kurkdjian, Parfums de Marly) are gathered, authenticated, and presented with editorial care.

The name was chosen because "treasure" carries exactly the meaning the platform needs to project:

1. **A treasure is found, not made.** The word inherently describes something *discovered and gathered*, never manufactured — which is precisely KENZ's relationship to the fragrance houses it presents. This protects the platform's single most important positioning rule: **KENZ is the curator, never a perfume house.**
2. **A treasure implies rarity and worth.** Every fragrance on the platform, from an accessible designer classic to a rare niche extrait, is framed as something worth discovering — not a commodity on a shelf.
3. **It carries genuine cultural and linguistic weight.** As an Arabic word rendered in a clean, four-letter Latin mark, KENZ is short, ownable, globally pronounceable, and free of the generic "Maison"/"Atelier"/"Vault" naming patterns common across luxury retail — while still being rooted in a real, meaningful language rather than an invented portmanteau.
4. **It scales beyond fragrance.** Because "treasure" describes a category of value rather than a product type, the name does not lock the brand into perfumery-only language should the marketplace ever extend into adjacent luxury categories.

All references to "the platform" or "the brand" throughout this document and its companion documents (`LOGO_SPEC.md`, `LOGO_CONCEPT.md`, `LOGO_USAGE.md`) refer to **KENZ**.

### 1.2 Platform Purpose

KENZ is a **curated, premium marketplace** for luxury and niche perfumery. It does not manufacture fragrance — it authenticates, curates, and sells fragrances produced by the world's most respected perfume houses. Its purpose is to be the single, trusted destination where a customer can discover, compare, and acquire fragrance across the full spectrum of the perfumery world — from accessible designer classics to rare niche extraits — without ever having to think about which individual brand's own website to visit.

This distinction is foundational and must never be blurred in messaging: **KENZ is the curator, not a perfume house.** Every piece of copy, every image, and every brand decision should reinforce "we found this treasure and are presenting it to you" rather than "we made this."

### 1.3 Brand Positioning

KENZ positions itself as **the editorial luxury marketplace of fragrance** — the category equivalent of what a Net-a-Porter, Ounass, or Mytheresa is to fashion. It sits at the intersection of three positions, and deliberately occupies all three at once:

| Position | What it means for the brand |
|---|---|
| **Authority** | A trusted, expert-vetted source — never a discount bazaar. Every listed house is presented with the same editorial care regardless of its retail price point. |
| **Curator** | A tastemaker that organizes an overwhelming category (thousands of SKUs, dozens of houses) into something legible, personal, and discoverable — through editorial storytelling, scent pyramids, and AI-assisted guidance. |
| **Concierge** | A white-glove service layer — the platform behaves like a knowledgeable in-store perfumer, not a search results page. |

KENZ is explicitly **not**: a discount outlet, a dupe/clone fragrance retailer, a single-brand storefront, a mass-market drugstore experience, a generic "everything marketplace," or — under any circumstances — a perfume manufacturer.

### 1.4 Value Proposition

> **"A treasure trove of the world's finest fragrance houses — curated, authenticated, and explained by experts, matched to you by AI."**

The value proposition rests on four pillars:

1. **Breadth without dilution.** Access to designer and niche houses side by side, curated to a single quality bar — customers never have to wonder if a listing is genuine or lesser-tier.
2. **Editorial expertise.** Every fragrance is explained, not just listed — notes, families, occasions, and season guidance accompany the product, mirroring the guidance of a skilled in-store perfumer.
3. **Personalized discovery.** The AI Scent Concierge and Scent Matchmaker quiz remove the paralysis of choosing among thousands of options, translating mood, occasion, and preference into a short, confident shortlist.
4. **Trust and service.** Premium packaging, transparent sourcing, responsive support, and a frictionless purchase experience — the parts of luxury retail that customers feel even when they can't articulate them.

## 5. Brand Personality

The brand personality is expressed through seven core traits. Each trait includes a short definition and a concrete "do / don't" to keep design and copy decisions consistent.

| Trait | Definition | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Elegant** | Restrained, considered, never loud. | Let whitespace and typography carry the weight of a message. | Stack multiple competing visual elements or use more than one accent color per screen. |
| **Premium** | Every detail signals quality and intentionality. | Use high-resolution studio photography and generous spacing even in dense views (e.g., cart, checkout). | Use stock photography that feels generic, or compress product imagery to save load time at the cost of visible quality. |
| **Minimal** | Says only what is necessary, shows only what matters. | Favor a single strong headline over three qualifying subheadings. | Add decorative UI chrome, badges, or banners that don't serve a functional or storytelling purpose. |
| **Editorial** | Reads like a well-produced fragrance publication, not a product catalog. | Frame collections and features as stories ("Sourced from Grasse," "The Olfactory Pyramid"). | Present products as a flat, undifferentiated grid with no narrative framing. |
| **Trustworthy** | Feels authenticated, transparent, and dependable. | Be explicit about sourcing, availability, and policies; keep promises (delivery estimates, stock status) accurate. | Use urgency tactics that feel manipulative (fake countdown timers, exaggerated "only 1 left" messaging) unless the data is genuinely real-time and accurate. |
| **Modern** | Contemporary in interaction and technology, timeless in aesthetic. | Use current interaction patterns (AI concierge, smooth motion, responsive design) inside a classic visual frame. | Chase visual trends (neon gradients, glassmorphism-for-its-own-sake) that will date quickly against the brand's timeless palette. |
| **Sophisticated** | Assumes an intelligent, discerning audience. | Write copy that respects the customer's expertise, even when explaining basics (see Section 6). | Talk down to the customer, over-explain, or use exclamation points and hype language. |

**Personality summary line (for internal briefs):** *KENZ behaves like the best-read, most soft-spoken specialist in the room — never the loudest, always the most trusted keeper of the collection.*

## 6. Tone of Voice

### 6.1 Principles

UI copy should read as though written by a knowledgeable specialty retailer, not a growth-marketing team. Four principles govern every string of copy on the platform:

1. **Confident, not salesy.** State facts and let quality speak. Avoid superlatives that aren't earned ("best-selling," "incredible") in favor of specific, verifiable detail ("a woody-aromatic composition built on cedar and vetiver").
2. **Warm, not casual.** The brand is approachable and human, but never uses slang, excessive exclamation points, or forced humor. Warmth comes from clarity and helpfulness, not informality.
3. **Precise, not verbose.** Every sentence should earn its place. Prefer one well-chosen sentence over three qualifying ones.
4. **Respectful of expertise on both ends.** Copy should serve both the newcomer ("What does EDP mean?") and the collector (precise concentration, batch, and note information) without patronizing either.

### 6.2 Voice in Practice — Examples

| Context | Off-Brand (avoid) | On-Brand (use) |
|---|---|---|
| Hero headline | "HUGE SALE! Luxury Perfumes Up To 50% Off!!!" | "Every Treasure Has Its Story." |
| Add to cart confirmation | "Yay! It's in your bag! 🎉" | "Added to your bag." |
| Low stock | "HURRY! Only 2 left!!! Don't miss out!!" | "Only 2 bottles remaining." |
| Empty wishlist | "Oops, nothing here yet! Go shopping now!" | "Your wishlist is currently empty." |
| Product description opener | "This amazing scent will blow you away!" | "A woody aromatic built on cedar, vetiver, and a whisper of citrus." |
| Newsletter CTA | "Sign up NOW for exclusive deals!" | "Receive invitations to new arrivals and private collection previews." |
| Error message | "Oops! Something broke. Try again I guess." | "Unable to load this page. Please try again." |

### 6.3 Voice Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Use precise sensory and technical vocabulary (concentration, notes, longevity, projection) accurately.
- Write in complete, well-formed sentences — even for short UI strings.
- Let product facts carry persuasive weight instead of adjectives.
- Address the customer directly and respectfully ("your order," "your wishlist").

**Don't:**
- Use more than one exclamation point anywhere on the platform, including marketing email.
- Use urgency or scarcity language that is not backed by real data.
- Use slang, emoji, or meme-adjacent phrasing in product or transactional copy.
- Apologize excessively in error states ("We're SO sorry!!") — a calm, single-sentence acknowledgment is more trustworthy.

## 7. Target Audience

The platform serves four overlapping but distinct audience segments. Every segment should feel served by the same experience — the platform does not fork its design or tone by segment, but content, recommendations, and marketing emphasis should flex to meet each group's motivation.

### 7.1 Segment Profiles

| Segment | Age Range | Motivation | What They Need From the Platform |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Considered Luxury Shopper** | 28–55 | Wants quality, provenance, and a frictionless premium experience across categories, not just fragrance. | Trust signals, authentication transparency, premium packaging/service, minimal friction at checkout. |
| **The Gift Buyer** | 25–65 | Shopping for someone else, often under time pressure, values presentation and confidence in the choice. | Clear gender-neutral framing, gift-set curation, confident guided recommendations, reliable delivery estimates. |
| **The Fragrance Enthusiast** | 22–45 | Actively building a personal fragrance wardrobe, enjoys learning about notes/families/seasons. | Rich olfactory content (scent pyramids, note breakdowns), season/occasion guidance, ability to compare across houses. |
| **The Niche Perfume Collector** | 25–50 | Deeply knowledgeable, seeks rare/niche/artisanal houses, skeptical of mass-market framing. | Access to niche houses presented with the same editorial seriousness as designer houses; precise, jargon-correct copy; no dumbing down. |

### 7.2 Cross-Segment Principles

- **Gender-neutral by default.** Fragrance is not marketed as "for him" / "for her" in primary navigation or hero content; gender-lean filters exist as a practical tool but are never the platform's primary organizing narrative.
- **No single segment is "core" at the expense of others.** A niche collector and a first-time gift buyer should both find the homepage credible — achieved through editorial tone and expert content that respects intelligence.
- **Global, English-first audience.** Per `ARCHITECTURE.md` §20, the platform is currently English-only/LTR. The KENZ name itself carries meaning across languages; copy elsewhere should avoid idioms that don't translate well if localization is added later.

## 8. Visual Identity

### 8.1 Visual Language

The visual language is **editorial minimalism with a metallic accent** — the "Minimalist Elegance & Editorial Sophistication" philosophy defined in `ARCHITECTURE.md` §2, applied here as brand doctrine rather than UI doctrine. Every visual decision should ask: *does this look like a page from a well-produced fragrance journal, or does it look like a product catalog?* The former is always correct.

Core visual principles:
- **Generous negative space** is used as a luxury signal, not filled out of a fear of "empty" screens.
- **One focal element per view.** A hero image, a single product, a single quote — never competing visual centers.
- **High contrast, low saturation.** The palette (Section 10) is built on near-black, warm white, and a single warm metallic accent — never bright, saturated color blocking.
- **Texture over ornament.** Where visual interest is needed, it comes from real material texture (glass, liquid, metal, paper) captured in photography — never from decorative UI graphics, gradients-for-their-own-sake, or illustrated flourishes.

### 8.2 Photography Style

- **Studio-lit, high-contrast product photography** on a dark slate or soft neutral base.
- **Macro liquid and glass texture shots** used for storytelling sections (heritage, craftsmanship) — close, tactile, almost abstract framing of the fragrance itself.
- **Editorial lifestyle photography**, used sparingly, always restrained in composition — never busy, brightly lit lifestyle "influencer" photography.
- **Consistent color grading** across all photography: warm-neutral highlights, deep (not crushed) shadows, no oversaturated color casts — every image should look like it belongs to the same visual family regardless of which house's product it depicts.

### 8.3 Image Composition

- **Rule of restraint, not rule of thirds alone.** Compositions favor centered or minimally-offset subjects with abundant surrounding negative space, reflecting the brand's editorial calm.
- **Consistent aspect ratios** per context so grids feel machine-precise and considered, never haphazard.
- **Directional light from a single consistent source** across a shoot, reinforcing the "studio" feel.

### 8.4 Product Photography

- Every product is shot **on the same neutral/dark base treatment** regardless of the originating house, so a Dior bottle and a Xerjoff bottle feel like they belong to the same curated treasury rather than two mismatched brand asset libraries.
- **No cropped, low-resolution, or manufacturer-supplied marketing banners** are used as primary product imagery.
- Bottle photography should show **true-to-life color and glass clarity** — accuracy is a trust signal.

### 8.5 Editorial Direction

Editorial content (heritage features, olfactory guides, seasonal stories) should read as if commissioned from a fragrance journalist, not written as marketing copy with a headline slapped on top. Each editorial piece should:
- Open with a specific, sensory, factual observation — not a generic superlative.
- Credit provenance and craft specifically (region, ingredient, technique) rather than vague claims of "luxury" or "excellence."
- Treat every house profiled — designer or niche — with equal editorial seriousness.

## 9. Logo Direction

This section provides **directional guidance only**. Full logo rules and the frozen concept live in `LOGO_SPEC.md` and `LOGO_CONCEPT.md` — this section states the brand rationale those documents implement.

### 9.1 Typography Direction

- The wordmark is set as **KENZ**, in an elegant, high-contrast serif — the same serif register used for display headings elsewhere (`Playfair Display`, per `Design_System.md` §1.2) — never the interface sans-serif, which is reserved for UI, not brand identity.
- Letterforms favor **elegant, high-contrast strokes** (thin/thick transitions), avoiding heavy slab or geometric sans treatments associated with tech/startup branding.
- Letter-spacing is **slightly expanded** at small sizes to preserve legibility and add a refined, engraved quality.

### 9.2 Symbol Direction

If a mark/symbol is developed alongside the wordmark, it must evoke **curation, discovery, and treasure — never a specific scent or bottle shape**, which would misrepresent the marketplace as a single house. Directions worth exploring (elaborated fully in `LOGO_SPEC.md` §6 and resolved in `LOGO_CONCEPT.md`):

- An **abstract geometric monogram** built from the letter K, rendered as a precise, faceted single-weight line.
- A **jewel-cut / gem-facet geometry** — a small number of angular planes suggesting a cut stone, evoking "treasure" without depicting an object.
- A **vault or keyhole abstraction**, reduced far enough that it never reads as a literal security device.

**Absolute constraint — this is the single most important rule in the mark's brief:** no literal perfume bottle, cap, atomizer, spray, droplet, floral, or fragrance-container iconography may be used as the primary symbol or anywhere in the wordmark lockup. This is the visual cue most likely to misrepresent the platform as a single-product brand rather than a curated marketplace. See `LOGO_SPEC.md` §10 for the full list of forbidden elements.

### 9.3 Spacing

- The wordmark (and symbol, if present) must be surrounded by a **minimum clear-space margin equal to the cap-height of the wordmark** on all sides in any lockup.
- In the navigation header (`Design_System.md` §3.17), the logo should never be crowded by adjacent icons or navigation links — preserve at least one full icon-width of breathing room on either side.

### 9.4 Usage

- Primary lockup: wordmark alone, set in `Canvas Dark / Noir` on light backgrounds and `Off-White / Soft Silk` on dark backgrounds.
- The **Champagne Gold** accent may be used for the symbol only (if one exists) or for a single accent element (e.g., a hairline rule beneath the wordmark) — never as the wordmark's default fill color across large surfaces, where it would read as decorative rather than authoritative.
- Minimum digital size should preserve legibility of the full wordmark at the smallest UI touchpoint (favicon/mobile nav) — a simplified symbol-only version is reserved for contexts too small for the full wordmark (browser tab icon, app icon).

### 9.5 Incorrect Usage

The logo must never be:
- Stretched, skewed, or set at a non-horizontal angle.
- Recolored outside the approved palette (Section 10) — no gradients, no arbitrary brand-adjacent colors.
- Placed on a busy photographic background without a scrim/overlay ensuring contrast compliance.
- Combined with a drop shadow, bevel, glow, or other decorative effect.
- Paired with a tagline in a different typeface than the one specified for taglines.
- Depicted using, or alongside, any perfume-bottle, spray, or droplet iconography (see `LOGO_SPEC.md` §10).
- Reproduced at a size where the wordmark becomes illegible rather than swapped for the simplified symbol.

## 10. Color Philosophy

The existing visual language is retained in full — the rebrand changes the name and narrative, not the palette.

| Role | Token (`Design_System.md` §1.1) | Role in the Brand |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary — Near Black** (`--color-neutral-900`, "Canvas Dark / Noir") | High-contrast anchor for the AI Concierge module, dark hero overlays, and footer. | Grounds the brand in seriousness and permanence; ideal stage for gold and product photography to read as premium. |
| **Secondary — Warm White** (`--color-neutral-0` / `--color-neutral-50`, "Off-White / Soft Silk") | Primary light-mode background. | Feels closer to fine paper stock or a boutique's interior than a clinical, tech-SaaS white. |
| **Accent — Champagne Gold** (`--color-primary-500` and scale, "Luxury Gold") | Reserved for CTAs, focus states, and signature highlights. | The only saturated color in the system — used sparingly, it reads as considered exclusivity, never decoration. **Gold is an accent only and is never the default fill color of the logo.** |
| **Neutral Slate / Charcoal** (`--color-neutral-600`–`800`) | Secondary text, card borders, dark-surface elements. | Provides depth and hierarchy without introducing a second hue, so dozens of different perfume houses' own branding can sit inside the platform without visual conflict. |
| **Muted Neutral** (`--color-neutral-300`–`400`) | Secondary text, placeholders, subtitles. | Signals restraint — informational text recedes so product photography and primary copy retain visual authority. |
| **Semantic Feedback Colors** | Functional states only (stock status, form validation, toasts). | Deliberately muted/desaturated so functional feedback never competes with the brand's core gold/noir/white palette. |

**Governing rule:** the palette is intentionally restrained to *near-monochrome plus one metallic accent*. This restraint is itself a brand statement — a marketplace confident enough in its curation does not need a colorful interface to hold attention. Any proposal to introduce an additional primary hue should be treated as a significant brand decision, not a routine design choice.

## 11. Typography Philosophy

The dual-typeface system defined in `Design_System.md` §1.2 (`Playfair Display` for display/headings, `Plus Jakarta Sans` for body/interface) is retained unchanged and continues to hold two competing needs in balance: **editorial elegance** and **e-commerce clarity**.

- **`Playfair Display` (serif, display/headings, and the KENZ wordmark)** carries the brand's *emotional* register — heritage, craftsmanship, and editorial authority. It is reserved for headlines, section titles, brand moments, and the wordmark itself — never for dense UI or transactional copy.
- **`Plus Jakarta Sans` (sans-serif, body/interface/forms)** carries the brand's *functional* register — clarity, modernity, and trustworthiness in every transactional moment (forms, pricing, cart, checkout).

**Why this pairing, specifically, matches the brand:** the serif/sans split mirrors KENZ's own dual identity as both *editorial curator* (serif moments: hero headlines, brand storytelling, olfactory pyramid section titles) and *trusted retailer* (sans moments: prices, buttons, form labels, order confirmations).

## 12. Iconography Style

- **Line-based, single-weight icons only** (Lucide Icons, per the technical stack) — no filled, duotone, or skeuomorphic icon styles.
- **Consistent stroke weight and corner radius** across every icon on the platform.
- Icons are used **strictly functionally** — navigation, actions, and status indicators (cart, wishlist heart, search, filters) — never as decorative section dividers or marketing graphics.
- Icon color follows the same restraint as the rest of the palette: **neutral by default**, with Champagne Gold reserved for active/selected states only.
- Custom perfumery-specific iconography (e.g., a scent-pyramid diagram, a concentration-strength indicator) should follow the same line weight and geometry as the base Lucide set.

## 13. Illustration Style

Illustration is used **sparingly and only in service of education**, never as decoration:

- **Diagrammatic, not decorative.** Reserved for explanatory content — the Olfactory Pyramid, a concentration-strength comparison, an application guide. No ornamental illustration, mascots, or whimsical iconography.
- **Line-art or subtle single-tone fills**, consistent with the iconography system — never full-color, cartoon-style, or heavily rendered illustration.
- **Botanical/material line studies** (e.g., a bergamot cross-section, a sandalwood branch) are the one area where slightly more detailed, engraving-style linework is appropriate — evoking classic naturalist/perfumery reference illustrations, never a literal bottle or spray.
- Illustration should never substitute for real product or editorial photography where photography is expected.

---

## 14. Motion Philosophy

Motion exists to **communicate confidence and precision**, not to entertain. The motion tokens defined in `Design_System.md` §1.6 and §5 encode this technically; the principle is unchanged by the rebrand:

1. **Motion should feel inevitable, not surprising** — deceleration-style easing, objects settle with weight, never bounce or overshoot.
2. **Duration signals scale, not delight** — micro-interactions resolve in 150ms; larger layout changes take 250–350ms.
3. **Restraint is the luxury signal** — motion clarifies a state change, never decorates.
4. **Motion must never compromise usability** — every animated component respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 15. Copywriting Guidelines

All UI copy follows the tone-of-voice principles in Section 6. Surface-specific rules:

### 15.1 Hero Sections
Lead with a short, declarative headline (5–8 words) in the display serif. One-sentence subhead maximum. Avoid stacked exclamation commands.

> **Example:**
> Headline: *"Every Treasure Has Its Story."*
> Subhead: *"A curated collection of the world's most respected fragrance houses, gathered in one place."*
> CTA: *"Explore the Collection"*

### 15.2 Buttons
Verb-first, specific labels. "Add to Cart," "Proceed to Checkout," "Save Address" — never marketing flourishes on functional buttons. Destructive actions labeled explicitly: "Remove," "Delete Address," "Cancel Order."

### 15.3 Product Descriptions
Open with the fragrance family and composition, not a subjective claim. Follow with occasion/season guidance. Never use unverifiable superlatives. Always disclose concentration (EDT/EDP/Parfum) and size clearly.

### 15.4 Empty States
State the situation plainly, offer one clear next action.

> **Example:** *"Your wishlist is currently empty. Explore the collection to find fragrances worth saving."* → **"Explore Catalog"**

### 15.5 Error Messages
State what happened, plainly, without jargon or blame. Always pair with a concrete next step.

> **Example:** *"Unable to load your order. Please try again, or contact support if the issue continues."*

### 15.6 Checkout
The most plainly functional copy on the platform — no editorial flourish. Every field label unambiguous; every cost line item labeled exactly. Order confirmation copy may return to the warmer editorial voice: *"Thank you, [Name]. Your order has been placed."*

### 15.7 AI Features (Scent Concierge / Matchmaker)
The AI communicates with the same restrained, expert tone as the rest of the brand — a knowledgeable in-store perfumer, not a chatty assistant. Recommendations always explain their reasoning in one or two factual sentences. The AI is transparent about uncertainty rather than forcing a recommendation.

## 17. Accessibility & Inclusivity

Accessibility is treated as a **brand value, not a compliance checkbox**:

- **Contrast is a brand discipline, not a constraint on aesthetics.** The near-monochrome-plus-gold palette (Section 10) was chosen specifically because it can meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast ratios while remaining visually restrained.
- **Never communicate meaning through color alone.** Stock status, order status, and form validation always pair color with text or iconography.
- **Typography respects legibility at every size.** The display serif (`Playfair Display`, including the KENZ wordmark) is reserved for headline-scale text; body and interface text always uses the more legible sans-serif.
- **Gender-neutral, inclusive framing throughout**, as stated in Section 7.2.
- **Motion respects vestibular sensitivity** — every animated brand moment has a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback.
- **Plain-language clarity benefits every customer** — the precision-without-jargon-overload principle (Section 6) is itself an accessibility practice.

## 19. Final Brand Summary

**KENZ** — Arabic for "Treasure" — is a premium, multi-brand fragrance marketplace: the curated, editorial destination where the world's most respected designer and niche perfume houses are presented side by side, explained with genuine expertise, and matched to each customer through thoughtful, AI-assisted guidance. It is a curator and custodian, never a manufacturer, and every brand decision — from the near-monochrome-plus-gold palette, to the serif/sans typographic split, to the restrained, precision-led tone of voice — exists to reinforce that single positioning: **an authoritative, trustworthy treasury for fragrance discovery, not a single house's storefront.**

**One-line creative brief:**
> *Design and write for KENZ as if producing a beautifully restrained fragrance journal that happens to let you buy what's on the page — confident, precise, editorial, and never louder than the craftsmanship it presents.*

**The five things every contributor should remember:**
1. We curate; we do not manufacture. Never let copy or imagery imply the platform created a fragrance.
2. Restraint is the luxury signal — in color, in motion, in copy length, in adjectives used.
3. Every house — designer or niche — receives equal editorial seriousness.
4. The name means "Treasure" — let that inform tone (discovery, worth, care) without over-using the word itself in copy.
5. Accessibility and inclusivity are expressions of the brand's sophistication, not constraints on it.
